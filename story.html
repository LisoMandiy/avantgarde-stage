The theatre / Context

The stage as a machine.

For over a decade the leading avant-garde painters left the easel for the stage, rebuilding it as construction, machine and architecture. Many of these productions survive only in design drawings and photographs — which is what this archive collects.

1913

Victory over the Sun

Malevich's Futurist opera splits the backcloth black and white — Suprematism is born on a stage.

1922

The machine-set

Popova's set for The Magnanimous Cuckold makes Constructivism the language of the theatre.

1920s

Painters on stage

Exter, Stepanova and Rodchenko treat the stage as architecture and machine, not painted backdrop.

1926–29

Biomechanics

Meyerhold's productions push the constructive stage to its limit before the avant-garde is shut down.

Alexandra Exter's cubist sets, Lyubov Popova's scaffolding for Meyerhold, and Malevich's founding opera treated theatre as a laboratory for the new art. Read together, the bills here trace how the avant-garde used the stage — a public, temporary, collaborative form — to test ideas that painting alone could not hold.
